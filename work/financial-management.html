What it covered

The four questions a finance function actually answers

How a firm should be funded, what that funding costs, which projects are worth doing with it, and how to keep enough cash moving to stay alive in the meantime. Capital structure, the cost of capital, investment appraisal and working capital management, which between them cover most of what a corporate finance team is for.

The appraisal work is the part I use most. Net present value and internal rate of return are simple to calculate and easy to misuse, because both are entirely hostage to the discount rate and the cash flow forecasts you feed them. A project can be made to look fundable by moving the cost of capital by a point, which is a good reason to interrogate the assumption rather than the output.

Why it is on this page

The honest version

This was a taught and examined module rather than a project, so there is no artefact to show you and I am not going to pretend otherwise. It is here because it is the grounding underneath the quantitative work elsewhere on this site, and because the roles I am applying for are the ones this module describes.

What I took from it is less the formulae than the habit of asking which assumption the answer is actually resting on. That shows up again in the endowment portfolio work, where the allocation turned out to depend heavily on how far back the return estimates were drawn from.
