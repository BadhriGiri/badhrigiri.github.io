Central claim
The efficiency gain and the accountability gap are the same mechanism

The central argument

Where the efficiency actually comes from

A smart contract is efficient because it executes without anyone deciding to execute it. That is the whole proposition. The uncomfortable implication, and the argument the portfolio is built on, is that the efficiency gain comes from removing human judgment, and human judgment is exactly what legal accountability frameworks are attached to.

Banking regulation works by being able to name the person responsible. Automate the decision and you have not removed the responsibility, you have removed the person it was attached to. Anti money laundering makes this concrete: a rule can be encoded, but the judgment about whether a pattern of behaviour is suspicious is not a rule, and encoding it does not make it one.

The immutability paradox

A strength that only works in one direction

Immutability is the property blockchain is sold on, and it is genuinely valuable: a record nobody can quietly alter afterwards is worth a great deal in a dispute.

It is also indiscriminate. An immutable ledger preserves a wrong input exactly as faithfully as a right one, and the correction has to live alongside the error rather than replacing it. The guarantee is about the record, not about the truth, and conflating those two is the most common error in the enthusiastic literature.

Two jurisdictions

A choice, not a technical conclusion

England and Wales and the European Union have taken visibly different routes, with the EU arriving at a comprehensive regime in MiCA, applying from December 2024, and England and Wales proceeding more incrementally.

The useful lens is Brummer and Yadav's innovation trilemma: a regulator can pursue clear rules, market integrity and financial innovation, but not all three at once, and whichever it sacrifices is a policy choice rather than a technical necessity. Read that way, the divergence between the two regimes is not one of them being ahead of the other. They have given up different things.

This is the module that directly fed the compliance work on Blockmediary, where the same tension between an immutable ledger and a legal right of erasure stopped being an essay question and became something I had to write a position on.
