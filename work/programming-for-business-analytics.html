The thread running through it

Four exercises, one habit

Each task was a small business scenario rather than an abstract puzzle: monthly sales for a retail chain, quarterly customer acquisition, employee working hours, a product catalogue. The code is not complicated. What was being assessed was whether you could justify the choice, not whether you could make it run.

The two choices worth explaining

Immutability, and picking the right chart

The sales exercise ended by converting the series to a tuple rather than leaving it as a list. That looks like a detail and it is a deliberate one: historical sales should not be modifiable by accident, and choosing an immutable structure makes a whole class of bug impossible rather than merely unlikely. It is the same instinct that later made the immutability of a blockchain interesting to me for the opposite reason.

The visualisation exercise asked for quarterly customer acquisition, and the honest answer is that it needs two charts. A bar chart compares magnitudes across discrete categories, a line chart shows a trend through time, and quarterly acquisition is plausibly either question. Choosing wrongly does not produce an error message, which is precisely why it is worth being deliberate about.

Object oriented design

Putting the rule where it cannot be skipped

The last exercise built a product class holding name, price and stock, with methods to apply a discount and to sell units. The useful part is that the class refuses to sell stock it does not have, so the business rule lives inside the object rather than in whatever code happens to call it.

That is a small example of a general principle: a constraint enforced in one place holds everywhere, and a constraint enforced by convention holds until somebody is in a hurry.
