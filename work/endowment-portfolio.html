Endowment portfolio

Investment recommendations for a university endowment fund under budget pressure, against a CFO target of 0.3% monthly return. A group report, and the part I would point at is the question of whether our answer was stable at all.

The brief

A return target and a shrinking budget

The University of Wakanda's endowment was being leaned on to cover budget shortfalls caused by falling student enrolment, and its CFO had set a target of 0.3% a month. The fund held a three ETF bond portfolio. Our job was to say whether it could reach the target and what it should hold instead.

The number matters more than it looks. A fund being drawn on to bridge a shortfall cannot absorb a bad year the way one with headroom can, so the brief was about the downside at least as much as the return.

What we built

The allocation, and the curve underneath it

Mean variance optimisation and efficient frontier analysis across an expanded universe of six bond ETFs, then a Black Litterman overlay so the team's own market views could be folded in without overwhelming the historical estimates. Yield curves built by bootstrapping, with bond valuation on top. Performance judged on return, volatility, Sharpe ratio and maximum drawdown rather than return alone.

Expanding the investment universe visibly improved the efficient frontier, and the defensive portfolio behaved defensively because of its weighting toward low volatility assets. None of that is surprising. The next part is the bit worth reading.

Where I pushed back

The answer depended on where we started looking

The portfolios were constructed from 96 monthly observations, January 2016 to December 2023. Mean variance optimisation is notoriously sensitive to its expected return inputs, and 96 observations is not many to estimate them from.

So we re-ran the construction on an expanded in-sample period of 150 observations and compared the weights and performance directly. With a standard error of roughly 0.004 on a sample that size, a portfolio whose composition moves materially when you extend the window is not giving you an allocation, it is giving you an artefact of the start date.

Running the comparison and publishing both versions is less satisfying than reporting one confident set of weights. It is also the only honest way to present an optimiser's output to somebody who intends to act on it.

Where it is unfinished

What I would check before anyone acted on it

  • Transaction costs and rebalancing frequency are not modelled. Any strategy that reweights trades, and trading is not free, so the reported figures are optimistic.
  • The investment universe is six bond ETFs because the brief set it there. A real endowment mandate would not be constrained to one asset class.
  • This was an assessed teaching case worked on over one term, not a live mandate. The analysis is careful and it has not been tested by anybody losing money on it.
