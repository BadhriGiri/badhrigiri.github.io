Semester one · September to December 2025

The foundation

BEAM047

Fundamentals of Financial Management

Corporate finance from the ground up: capital structure, the cost of capital, investment appraisal and working capital. My strongest module of the year, and the one that sits closest to where I want to work.

BEEM011

Applied Econometrics

A panel study of household savings behaviour in the United Kingdom: 540 observations across 90 individuals over six waves. I specified the model before opening the data and argued each variable in, then extended it with a cubic income term, interactions between income, family and gender, and individual and time effects. Written in R.

  • Available on request

BEEM061

Fundamentals of Financial Technology

Four exercises in sequence: exploring the Bitcoin blockchain as a live dataset through its API, a time series investigation, reproducing a published machine learning model from its description, and then building my own credit default classifier. Reproducing someone else's model first is what taught me how much of a stated result lives in the choices that never made it into the write up.

  • Available on request

The working toolkit in Python: data structures and when immutability matters, choosing a chart that answers the question actually asked, functions, and object oriented design. Unglamorous, and it is what everything else on this page runs on.

Semester two · January to April 2026

Modelling, banking, strategy and law

BEAM046

Financial Modelling

Investment recommendations for a university endowment fund under budget pressure, with a target of 0.3% monthly return. I was lead quantitative analyst in a six person team: mean variance optimisation and efficient frontier analysis, a Black Litterman overlay to fold market views into the allocation, yield curves built by bootstrapping and Nelson Siegel fitting, and bond valuation on top of them. Performance judged on return, volatility, Sharpe ratio and maximum drawdown.

The part I would point at first is the out of sample work: rolling window statistical arbitrage and estimation bias analysis lifted the out of sample Sharpe ratio from 0.41 to 0.56 and cut maximum drawdown from 1.23% to 0.98%. An in sample result that does not survive out of sample testing is not a result.

  • Available on request

BEEM062

Advanced Financial Technology

An essay on how blockchain systems should be evaluated and compared, covering signature schemes and quantum resilience, consensus mechanisms and ledger state models, plus three technical pieces: gradient descent implemented from first principles on Bitcoin and Dogecoin price data, an Ethereum forecasting exercise using time series cross validation to avoid look ahead bias, and a dictator game deployed as a live smart contract.

  • Available on request

BEAM033

Banking and Financial Services

Financial intermediation and institutional roles under elevated rates and tightened liquidity: how commercial banks, insurers, pension funds and mutual funds each perform a different transformation, and what happens to each of them when funding conditions turn. Followed by an applied analysis and critical reflection.

  • Available on request

BEEM159

Law for FinTech

An essay arguing that culture, governance and compliance are three expressions of one question: whether people follow the rules when nobody is watching. It reads Lehman, Wirecard and Silicon Valley Bank as cultural failures that corrupted governance and left compliance superficial, rather than as cases of inadequate rules.

  • Available on request

BEMM190

Digital Transformation

A portfolio built on Lloyds Banking Group as a case of transformation at FTSE 100 scale: a visual analysis of the challenges and the strategic response, a career pathway piece on what working inside that transformation looks like, and a reflection on the learning and on using generative AI as part of it.

  • Available on request

LAWM183

International Banking and FinTech Law and Regulation

Whether blockchain can transform banking, and how regulators in England and Wales and the European Union are approaching its use in compliance and risk management. Assessed as a recorded presentation on smart contracts and banking law plus a reflective report. The slides are here as a PDF; the recorded voiceover version is available on request.

  • Available on request
